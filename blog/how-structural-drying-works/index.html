Understanding Professional Structural Drying in Naperville, IL

Home / Blog / The Science of Structural Drying in Naperville, IL

Ask people around Naperville, IL what restoration involves and drying the structure rarely comes up, although it is the core of the work. Once the visible water is gone, a lot is still held in the building itself, in framing lumber, drywall, insulation, subflooring, and concrete. Mold, lingering smells, and deteriorating materials are the result when the trapped water is not taken out correctly. This article explains the science behind professional structural drying and why it produces far better results than DIY attempts.

What Psychrometry Means for Drying

Psychrometry, a science concerned with gas-vapor mixtures and their thermodynamic and physical properties, is what structural drying is built on. For a drying job, that comes down to knowing how relative humidity, airflow, and temperature combine to move moisture out of a wet structure. With psychrometric calculations, an IICRC-certified technician works out the setup for a given job: how many dehumidifiers and which type, where the air movers go, and what temperature and humidity to aim for.

Commercial Drying Equipment

Professional drying gear is in a different class from anything sold for home use. A residential dehumidifier pulls 2 to 3 pints of water from the air each hour, while an LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) unit can pull 15 to 30 or more. Evaporation is sped up by air movers that deliver a directed, high-velocity stream of 2,500 CFM or more across wet surfaces. Under extreme moisture loads or in cold weather, the specialized tool is a desiccant dehumidifier. This equipment represents tens of thousands of dollars in investment and requires specific training to deploy effectively.

How a Drying Job Unfolds

Professional structural drying follows a systematic process. It begins with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which are used to map every area the water reached. From that map come the baseline readings and the plan for where equipment goes. Next, psychrometric calculations decide the position of each air mover and dehumidifier so that water evaporates and is removed efficiently. Adding heat is an option when a quicker evaporation rate is needed. A technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment as needed, and document progress. Equipment stays until readings for all affected materials come acceptably close to dry reference standards.

How Many Days Will Drying Take?

Expect 3 to 5 days for the majority of residential jobs when proper commercial gear is used. How long it takes comes down to how much moisture there is, which materials got wet (drywall dries far faster than concrete), how the building is constructed, the ambient conditions, and how many machines of what type are running. Wood framing typically dries in 3 to 4 days, standard drywall in 2 to 3 days, and concrete in 5 to 7 days or more. After the first moisture readings, the technician gives you an estimated schedule and updates it as the job moves along.

The Reason We Monitor Every Day

Without a daily check, drying is only a guess. Without calibrated moisture readings, there is no way to know whether materials are actually drying, drying at the expected rate, or have reached safe moisture levels. Deep in a material, moisture can remain at a dangerous level even though the surface feels dry when touched. Verifying it takes a moisture meter that has been calibrated. There is a paperwork benefit too: documented drying proves to an insurance company that the job met industry protocol, and that backs up your claim and your home's value.
